## 1. The short version

- FoolsGold runs on your Mac. It reads your mail to sort, summarise and prioritise it.
- We (the publisher) do **not** operate a server. We receive none of your mail, contacts or usage data.
- Your mail leaves your Mac only if **you** turn on a cloud AI provider or a network mail account. FoolsGold asks you before it does this, and you can switch it off at any time.

## 2. What FoolsGold processes, and where

| Data | Where it comes from | Where it is kept | Leaves your Mac? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email headers, bodies, attachments metadata | Apple Mail's local store on your Mac, or an account you connect | Local database in the FoolsGold data folder on your Mac | Only as described in section 3 |
| Priority verdicts, summaries, calendar suggestions, drafts | Generated by FoolsGold | Same local database | No |
| Settings and API keys you enter | You | Local database in `~/.foolsgold`; API keys encrypted at rest with a key stored beside it (moving it to the macOS Keychain is planned). The GitHub Copilot sign-in is held in the macOS Keychain | Keys are sent only to the provider they belong to |
| Crash or analytics data | Not collected | — | — |

Processing that stays on your device is not "collection" in the sense used by Apple's App Privacy labels.

## 3. When data is shared with third parties

1. **Cloud AI (optional, off by default).** FoolsGold supports three cloud providers: Anthropic ([privacy policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy)), OpenAI or any OpenAI-compatible server you name ([OpenAI privacy policy](https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy/)), and GitHub Copilot ([GitHub privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement)). If you choose one, FoolsGold sends, for each message being ranked or summarised, the sender, recipients, date, subject and up to about 3,600 characters of the body, plus your priority topics, over an encrypted connection, to produce priorities and your briefing.

   **Before the first request FoolsGold shows who receives the data, in which country, what is sent, why, how long it is kept, and what happens if you refuse, and it sends nothing until you press Allow.** The permission is tied to that provider and server: switching provider, or pointing the OpenAI setting at a different server, asks again. A server on your own Mac (e.g. `localhost`) is treated as local and never asks. Refusing keeps FoolsGold on structural ranking or the on-device AI (Ollama); everything else keeps working. You can withdraw at any time in Settings > AI provider > Withdraw permission, which stops transfers from the next request. FoolsGold keeps a local record of when you allowed and withdrawn permission.
2. **Local AI (Ollama).** Runs on your Mac. Nothing is sent off the device.
3. **Mail accounts you connect** (e.g. Microsoft 365 / SMTP). FoolsGold talks directly to your mail provider to read or send mail you ask it to.

These providers may be located outside your country (for example in the United States). Where Korean PIPA applies, this section is the notice of overseas transfer: recipient, country, items transferred (message text), purpose (analysis you requested), and retention (per the provider's terms). You can refuse by not enabling cloud AI; the core features still work.

We do not sell personal data and do not use it for advertising or tracking.

## 4. Use of AI

FoolsGold uses AI to rank and summarise mail. AI-generated priorities, reasons and summaries are labelled in the app. They may be wrong, so check important messages yourself. (Korea AI Basic Act transparency notice.)

## 6. Security

The local database stays in your user account; secrets are encrypted at rest; network connections use TLS. The app does not open network ports to other machines (its local service listens on 127.0.0.1 only).
