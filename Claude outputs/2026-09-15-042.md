# Mobile port: the cheap path was offered and refused, and the sequence was rewritten

## What was proposed

Four decisions were put to Danny before any plan was written: where mobile mail
comes from, which stack, how it reaches the phone, and what this session should
produce.

On the first, four options were offered and one was **explicitly marked as the
recommendation**: keep the FastAPI backend on the Mac, reach it over Tailscale,
make the iPhone a thin client. The case made for it was reuse — 5,092 lines of
Python, 209 tests, the Apple Mail source and the only LLM provider ever proven
to work, all retained, standing up inside a day. A fourth option was offered as
the hedge ("Mac first, cloud later"), which is the same recommendation wearing
a roadmap.

On the third, three distribution options were offered: free 7-day personal
provisioning, the $99 developer programme with TestFlight, or App Store
release. All three were about *shipping*.

## What Danny did

**He took the most expensive option on the first question** — a full on-device
rewrite — which discards essentially all of the reuse the recommendation was
built on. He gave no justification and did not argue with the cost estimate.

**He refused the frame on the third.** None of the three offered answers was
chosen. He answered a different question: *"일단 모바일 환경에서 구동되는지
여부부터 확인하자, 배포 및 출시는 나중에"* — first establish that it runs on
mobile at all; shipping comes later.

**He refused the frame on the fourth as well.** Four deliverables were offered
(plan doc, skills checklist, design mockups, start scaffolding). He selected
none of them and redirected the output to the ontology vault and its
presentation style.

## The diff

The proposal optimised for **cost of construction** and treated distribution as
a decision to be made up front, because it constrains the build. Danny inverted
both. He accepted a large construction cost without negotiating it, and refused
to make the distribution decision at all until the cheapest possible piece of
evidence — does an Expo app run on his iPhone — exists.

Two of the three refusals were not disagreements with the options. They were
rejections of the *question's* right to be asked yet.

Read against the existing project documents, this is consistent rather than
novel: `release-readiness.md` opens not with what works but with a table headed
**"what has never been exercised"**, and `rendering-differences.md` is an
account of a wrong diagnosis reached by skipping a verification step. The
pattern in this session and the pattern in those documents may be the same one.

## Recorded consequence

The plan written this session leads with phase **P0 — "Does it run"**: Expo Go,
fake data, no OAuth, no database, no model. It exists because of the third
answer, not because it was in the original proposal.
